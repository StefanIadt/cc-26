01 / The gap

The gap

Every Irish computing degree clusters in the same place – deep technical, low creative ambition. The space IADT could occupy is empty.

* How we scored these programmes +

Each of the 37 programmes was scored 1–10 on two axes, based on a review of publicly available module lists, programme descriptions and learning outcomes. Scores reflect the overall character of the degree – not the presence or absence of any single module.

Technical depth (X axis) measures how much of the degree is dedicated to writing, understanding and debugging real code – programming languages, data structures and algorithms, systems programming, networking, databases and computer architecture. A score of 9–10 indicates significant CS theory: compilers, formal methods, concurrent systems. A score of 7–8 indicates strong applied programming. A score of 5–6 indicates solid coding foundations where programming is not the dominant activity.

Creative ambition (Y axis) measures how much of the degree explicitly teaches design thinking, visual communication, human-centred process, prototyping and critique culture. A token UX or HCI module does not raise this score. A score of 7–8 requires design to be a named, sustained pillar across multiple years.

* IADT Creative Computing is scored 6 for technical depth. Students write real code across all four years – programming, databases, APIs, full-stack projects, cloud and networking. The score is not higher because the programme lacks the CS theory depth of university degrees: algorithms at scale, systems programming, compilers and formal methods.
