# CC 26 — Programme Model
## A Deliverables-First Framework for IADT Creative Computing
### A deliverables-first, sprint-based Level 8 degree

---

## The core idea

Start at the end. What does a graduate make in Year 4?
Work backwards. Every sprint, every module, every year builds toward that.

This model structures learning around deliverables — not topics.
Students don't study "databases." They build something that needs a database.
The sprint creates the reason to learn.

This document maps a sprint structure onto the existing
Creative Computing programme — keeping what works, naming what needs
to change, and making the pedagogy consistent rather than lecturer-dependent.

---

## The behavioural thread

Five behavioural dimensions run through every semester at increasing autonomy.
This is the most important structural idea — it makes graduate attributes
assessable and visible, not implicit.

Adapted for IADT:

| Dimension | What it means here |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Making** | Can design, build, test and ship a real thing |
| **Collaborating** | Can work in a team, give and receive honest feedback |
| **Communicating** | Can present, defend and document their work |
| **Problem-solving** | Can analyse a brief, make decisions, iterate |
| **Learning** | Can identify gaps in their own knowledge and fill them |

Each dimension is assessed at four levels across the four years:

- **Year 1** — Follow given approaches. Work with guidance.
- **Year 2** — Begin to make independent choices. Justify decisions.
- **Year 3** — Lead within a stream. Organise own work and team.
- **Year 4** — Full autonomy. Client-facing. Self-directed.

## Year 1 — Making things
### The spine. No streams. Everyone together.

**The goal:** Confidence and competence through making.
A student who arrives at 250 CAO points and has never written a line of code
leaves Year 1 having shipped two real things and defended them in front of peers.

**The deliverables:**

| Sprint | What students make | Module it lives in |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | A personal profile — designed in Figma, built in HTML/CSS | Digital Media Prototyping + Web Design |
| 2 | A responsive webpage for a real brief | Digital Media Prototyping + Web Design |
| 3 | A simple working game (Frogger-style) | Programming |
| 4 | A game with user interaction and UI | Programming |
| 5 | A database-driven web app | Web Development + Databases |
| 6 | Integrated Project — a full digital product combining all Y1 skills | Integrated Project |

**Sprint rhythm (same every sprint):**
Brief → Build → Review → Retrospect

The review is the non-negotiable. Every sprint ends with students
showing their work to peers and lecturers. Not submitting it. Showing it.

**Existing modules mapped:**

| Current module | Role in sprint model | Status |
|----------------|---------------------|--------|
| Digital Media Prototyping + Web Design (10cr) | Sprints 1–2 | Keep — already sprint-based, strong |
| Programming (10cr) | Sprints 3–4 | Keep — already sprint-based, games approach works |
| Web Development (10cr) | Sprint 5 | Keep — practical, real |
| Integrated Project (10cr) | Sprint 6 — capstone | Keep and strengthen as explicit sprint culmination |
| Introduction to Databases (5cr) | Feeds Sprint 5 | Review — consider folding into Web Development |
| Computing Concepts (5cr) | Background knowledge | Reimagine — make contextual to what students are building |
| FFACT Connect (5cr) | Institutional — induction | Keep, minimise |
| FFACT Skills Workshop (5cr) | Institutional | Keep, minimise |

**Behavioural criteria at Year 1 level:**
- Making: Follow given approaches. Build to specification. Test and document.
- Collaborating: Work in pairs and small groups. Give feedback in crits.
- Communicating: Present work clearly. Explain decisions at a basic level.
- Problem-solving: Follow a given brief. Identify blockers. Ask for help.
- Learning: Name what you don't know. Reflect after each sprint.

## Year 2 — Understanding things
### Still together. Streams hinted at. Depth increases.

**The goal:** Students begin to understand *why* things work, not just *how* to build them.
UX process, creative coding, software architecture. The stream split becomes visible
but is not formalised yet — students self-select through elective choices.

**The deliverables:**

| Sprint | What students make | Module it lives in |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 7 | A UX-researched redesign of an existing product | UX + Interaction Design |
| 8 | A designed, prototyped and tested interface | UX + Interaction Design |
| 9 | A creative coding experiment — generative, interactive or data-driven | Creative Coding |
| 10 | An advanced web application with real data | Advanced Web Development |
| 11 | A framework-built site or app (Tailwind, React or similar) | Web Design Frameworks |
| 12 | Software Project — a more complex system built end-to-end | Software Project |

**Existing modules mapped:**

| Current module | Role in sprint model | Status |
|----------------|---------------------|--------|
| UX + Interaction Design (10cr) | Sprints 7–8 | Keep — core to both streams |
| Creative Coding (10cr) | Sprint 9 | Keep — strong, relevant to Frontend + Experience stream |
| Advanced Web Development (10cr) | Sprint 10 | Keep |
| Web Design Frameworks (5cr) | Sprint 11 | Keep — extend to 10cr or combine |
| Software Project (10cr) | Sprint 12 capstone | Keep and restructure as explicit sprint |
| Advanced Databases (5cr) | Feeds Sprint 10/12 | Reassess — may be redundant if covered in Y1 |
| Architecture & Operating Systems (5cr) | Background for Backend stream | Keep — signals the engineering track |
| FFACT Elective (5cr) | Cross-faculty exposure | Keep — IADT's unique advantage |

**Behavioural criteria at Year 2 level:**
- Making: Begin to make independent choices. Combine given principles with own decisions.
- Collaborating: Take responsibility for own work. Give structured feedback to peers.
- Communicating: Present design decisions. Document professionally.
- Problem-solving: Analyse a brief independently. Sketch before building. Iterate.
- Learning: Follow field developments. Identify own knowledge gaps. Share with peers.

## Year 3 — Choosing a direction
### Streams split. Real work. Placement.

**The goal:** Students commit to a specialism and go deep.
Both streams continue the sprint model. Both do crits.
The difference is in what they build.

**Semester 1 — Stream-specific sprints**

### Frontend + Experience stream

| Sprint | What students make |
|--------|-------------------|
| 13 | A data visualisation — complex, considered, interactive |
| 14 | A physical computing project — sensors, hardware, real-world interaction |
| 15 | An immersive or spatial experience — XR, installation, or generative system |
| 16 | Advanced frontend — component library, design system, or complex interface |

Modules: Data Visualisation, Interactive Graphics, Front-End Development,
Physical Interaction, VR/AR

### Backend + Systems stream

| Sprint | What students make |
|--------|-------------------|
| 13 | A full-stack application with real architecture decisions |
| 14 | A mobile application |
| 15 | A back-end system with API design, security and scalability |
| 16 | A DevOps pipeline — CI/CD, deployment, monitoring |

Modules: Back-End Development, Mobile Computing, Games Development,
Networks + Cloud Computing (+ DevOps)

**Semester 2 — Placement**
Both streams. 20 ECTS. Sprint retrospects continue remotely.
Students document their placement work as a sprint portfolio.

Supporting modules both streams:
- Business + Entrepreneurship for Creative Practice
- Emerging Technologies + Trends

**Behavioural criteria at Year 3 level:**
- Making: Flexibly apply principles. Validate with real users or stakeholders.
- Collaborating: Organise team work. Manage own deliverables and others'.
- Communicating: Present convincingly to external audience. Document to professional standard.
- Problem-solving: Analyse complex problems. Choose and justify solution direction.
- Learning: Track field developments independently. Make own development choices.

## Year 4 — Mastery
### Streams converge on the Major Project.

**The goal:** One significant piece of work.
Not an assignment. A thing that exists in the world.

**Semester 1**

| Sprint | What students make |
|--------|-------------------|
| 17 | Research phase — problem definition, literature, methodology |
| 18 | Prototype — first working version, tested with real users or stakeholders |
| 19 | Iteration — refined version based on feedback |
| 20 | Major Project — final submission, public exhibition, defended in crit |

Supporting modules:
- Research + Analytics (10cr) — mandatory both streams
- Stream electives: Advanced JavaScript / Applied Data Science / AI / VR+AR / Physical Interaction

**Semester 2**
- Major Project — final sprint, public crit, On Show exhibition
- Professional Development + Critical Thinking

**The On Show exhibition IS the final sprint review.**
It is not a separate event bolted onto the end of the programme.
It is the moment the sprint model has been building toward since Year 1.

**Behavioural criteria at Year 4 level (full autonomy):**
- Making: Autonomous. Client or self-directed. Production quality.
- Collaborating: Lead. Manage stakeholders. Account for team results.
- Communicating: Persuasive. Professional. Public.
- Problem-solving: Diagnose, sketch, implement complex systems independently.
- Learning: Self-directed. Field-aware. Knows own limits and manages them.

## The deliverables — working backwards

If we start at the end and ask "what should a Year 4 student be able to make?"
the answer shapes everything that comes before it.

### Frontend + Experience stream — Year 4 graduate can make:
- A complex interactive data visualisation with real data
- An immersive or spatial experience using WebXR, physical computing or generative systems
- A production-quality design system with documentation
- A user-tested, accessible, performant web product

### Backend + Systems stream — Year 4 graduate can make:
- A full-stack application with thoughtful architecture
- A deployed, monitored system with CI/CD pipeline
- An AI-integrated application
- A mobile application with real users

### Both streams — Year 4 graduate can:
- Present and defend their work to a public audience
- Write about what they made and why
- Reflect honestly on what didn't work and what they'd do differently
- Point to four years of documented sprint work as evidence of their development

## What this needs to work

**Staff**
- Sprint-based teaching is already happening in Years 1, 3 and 4
- The gap is Year 2 — needs consistent crit culture and sprint rhythm
- New staff are the opportunity — involve them in designing the Year 2 sprint structure

**Structure**
- Each semester needs a named capstone sprint — a moment where everything comes together
- The Integrated Project in Year 1 already does this — strengthen and make it explicit
- The Software Project in Year 2 could do this — currently unclear if it does

**Clients**
- IADT uses staff as clients from Sprint 2.
- This works — continue it. Consider bringing in industry contacts for Year 3 sprints.
- The Research Assistant is building an industry contact list — this is the opportunity.

**The name**
- "Creative Computing" is a problem. Students and staff know it.
- The stream names — Frontend + Experience and Backend + Systems — are more legible.
- The programme review research will surface what prospective students and parents respond to.
- Do not rename until the research is in.
